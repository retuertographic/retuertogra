The logo is the most visible part of your [[visual identity|identidad-visual]], which is why it's hard to decide when to change it. Refreshing it for no reason can throw away years of built-up recognition; failing to do so when the time comes can make you look dated. The key is telling a real problem apart from a simple urge for something new.

## Signs that it's time for a refresh

- **It doesn't work at small sizes**: it becomes illegible as a social media profile picture, an app icon or in embroidery.
- **It doesn't adapt to today's formats**: it was designed for print and has no versions for dark backgrounds, square formats or screens.
- **Your business has changed**: new services, a different audience or a merger that the logo no longer represents.
- **It gets confused with another company's** in your sector or your area.
- **You don't have the original files**: it only exists as a low-quality image and every printer redraws it their own way.

## Refine or redesign

You don't always need to start from scratch. A **refinement** keeps the essence (symbol, colours, structure) and fixes what doesn't work: simplifying details, adjusting the typeface, creating horizontal and vertical versions. It's the right choice when your logo is well known and the problem is technical. A full **redesign** makes sense when the brand no longer represents the company or when its image carries recognition problems.

## How to make the change

1. **Analyse what works**: which elements people recognise and are worth keeping.
2. **Define versions** for every use: main, reduced, symbol only, positive and negative.
3. **Update the [[brand guidelines|manual-marca]]** with the new rules of use.
4. **Plan the rollout**: website, social media, stationery, signage and vehicles. Changing everything at once avoids a mixed image.
5. **Tell your customers about the change**, especially if it's a major redesign.

## What a logo can't do on its own

A new logo won't fix a poor customer experience or inconsistent communication. [[Branding|branding]] is the whole package: how you speak, how you look after people and how they see you at every point of contact. The logo is the signature on all of that.
