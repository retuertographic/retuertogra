The card is the basic unit of any food project that publishes content. It might be a recipe, a product you sell or a service such as catering or a cookery class. If every card follows the same structure, the content stays organised behind the scenes and looks consistent to visitors. If each one is different, visitors get lost and publishing takes you twice as long.

## The recipe card

A useful recipe answers the questions readers have before they start cooking:

- **A clear title**, phrased the way someone would search for it ("Papas arrugadas with red mojo" rather than "House classic").
- **A main photo** of the finished dish.
- **A short description**: where it comes from, when it's eaten, what makes it special.
- **Servings, preparation time and difficulty.**
- **Ingredients with quantities**, always written the same way in every recipe.
- **Numbered steps**, one per action, with photos or video of the tricky bits.
- **A category** (starter, dessert, seasonal…) so people can filter.
- **Allergens or notes**, where relevant.

## The product card

For food products (a sauce, a cheese, a tasting box) some fields change:

- Product name and photo.
- Description: origin, how it's made, flavour.
- Ingredients and format.
- Related recipes that use it.
- How to get it or whom to ask.

## The service card

A catering service, a workshop or an events menu needs, above all, to tell readers what's included and how to request it:

- What's included and what isn't.
- How many people or in what format.
- Photos from previous editions.
- A contact or request form asking only for the essentials.

## The form: the step that converts

A beautiful card without a clear way to get in touch is a missed opportunity. Add a visible call to action ([[CTA|cta]]), such as "Request information", and a short form: name, contact details, date and message. Ask only for what you need and include the privacy notice required by [[GDPR|rgpd]].

## Help Google find them

Each card has its own page, which is an opportunity for [[SEO|seo]]:

1. Put the [[keyword|palabra-clave]] someone would search for in the title ("green mojo recipe", not just "mojo").
2. Write your own description; don't copy another website's.
3. Give photos descriptive file names and alt text.
4. Link related recipes and products to each other.

## Common mistakes

- Cards with no photo, or photos of different sizes that break the layout.
- Quantities in different units depending on the recipe.
- Text copied from the packaging or other websites.
- Cards for dishes or services that no longer exist and nobody removes.
