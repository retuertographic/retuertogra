Almost every business handles personal data: a customer record, the phone number of someone making a booking, the email of someone asking for a quote or the team's payslips. From that moment on, the [[GDPR|rgpd]] (the EU General Data Protection Regulation) applies and, in Spain, so does the Organic Law on Data Protection and Guarantee of Digital Rights (LOPDGDD). Size doesn't matter: a freelancer with a small client list is bound by it too.

## Who it applies to

Any company or professional that collects, stores or uses data about identifiable people in the course of their business: customers, patients, students, suppliers who are individuals, or employees. Whoever decides why and how that data is used is the **controller**, and it's the controller who must comply.

## The basic obligations

1. **Have a legal basis for each use of the data.** For example, the contract with the customer, a legal obligation (such as invoicing) or consent, which in many cases is required for uses such as sending advertising.
2. **Inform people.** When you collect data, you have to explain who processes it, why, how long it's kept and how people can exercise their rights. This is usually done through notices on forms, in contracts and in the website's privacy policy.
3. **Keep a record of processing activities**, a document describing what data you process, for what purpose and how you protect it.
4. **Sign contracts with processors**: suppliers that access data on your behalf, such as your accountant, [[hosting|hosting]], management software or [[email marketing|email-marketing]] tool.
5. **Apply appropriate security measures**: strong passwords, access limited to those who need it, [[backups|copia-seguridad]] and up-to-date devices.
6. **Respond to people's rights**: access, rectification, erasure, objection, restriction and portability. As a general rule, you must reply within one month.
7. **Handle security breaches.** If data is lost or exposed and there's a risk to the people concerned, it must be reported to the Spanish Data Protection Agency (AEPD) within 72 hours of becoming aware of it.

Some organisations, depending on their activity or the volume and type of data they handle, must also appoint a data protection officer. It's not usual for a small shop, but it's worth checking.

## Checklist to get started

| Question | Yes / No |
| --- | --- |
| Do I know what personal data I handle and why? | ☐ |
| Do my forms and contracts explain how data is processed? | ☐ |
| Do I have a record of processing activities? | ☐ |
| Do I have a contract with every supplier that accesses data? | ☐ |
| Do I know how to respond if a customer asks for their data or to be deleted? | ☐ |
| Do I know what to do if data is lost or leaked? | ☐ |

## It's not a one-off task

The documentation has to reflect how the business actually works. If you take on a new supplier, open an online shop or start sending newsletters, it needs updating. That's why it's worth reviewing at least once a year.
