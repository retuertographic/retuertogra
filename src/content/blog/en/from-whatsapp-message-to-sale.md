Replying quickly to a WhatsApp message is good, but it isn't enough. Many opportunities are lost after the first reply: the customer asks for a quote, it gets sent, and nobody writes to them again. Weeks later, nobody remembers whether they accepted it, compared it with another one or simply forgot. The answer is to treat every conversation as the start of a process, not as an isolated message.

## The problem with managing sales from a mobile phone

When WhatsApp is run from a phone, information ends up scattered: some chats are on the office mobile, others on a team member's personal phone, and the quotes are in someone's email. There's no way of knowing how many enquiries came in this month, how many became customers or which ones are still waiting for a reply.

## What a CRM brings

A [[CRM|crm]] brings every contact and their history together in one place: what they asked, what they were offered and where things stand. Connected to [[WhatsApp Business|whatsapp-business]], every new conversation creates or updates the customer's record automatically. That way you can:

- **See every conversation** from the whole team in a shared inbox.
- **Assign each enquiry** to the person who should handle it.
- **Mark its status**: new, quote sent, negotiating, won or lost.
- **Schedule reminders** to get back in touch on the agreed date.

## Design your funnel

That path through the different statuses is your [[sales funnel|embudo]]. It doesn't need to be complicated: four or five stages that reflect how you really sell are enough. What matters is that the whole team uses the same stages and keeps them up to date. A [[chatbot|chatbot]] can take care of the first part, collecting the details and sorting the enquiry, so the person who picks it up already knows what it's about.

## The follow-up that makes the difference

1. **Reply the same day**, even if it's only to confirm that you're preparing the proposal.
2. **After sending a quote**, schedule a reminder a few days later to ask whether they have any questions.
3. **If the customer says no**, note down why. Over time you'll know whether you're losing sales on price, on lead times or for some other reason.
4. **Once the sale is closed**, keep the information for the future: company anniversaries, renewals or complementary services.

## What you'll see after a few months

With your data in order, things you used to go on gut feeling become clear: which services are most in demand, at what time of year most enquiries arrive and what percentage end in a sale. Those [[KPIs|kpi]] help you decide where to invest time and money.
