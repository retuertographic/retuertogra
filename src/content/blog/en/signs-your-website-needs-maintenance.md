Websites rarely break all at once. Usually they deteriorate little by little until one day something important stops working. These are the signs that your website needs attention, and they're best not ignored.

## 1. It takes a long time to load

A slow website loses visitors and rankings on Google. The most common causes are images that are far too heavy, a build-up of add-ons that are no longer used or [[hosting|hosting]] that's no longer up to the job. [[Core Web Vitals|core-web-vitals]] give you an objective measure of the problem.

## 2. Form messages don't arrive

This is one of the costliest faults because it's silent. If you haven't received any requests through your website for a while, send a test form. Changes to the mail server or to an add-on can stop messages being sent without any warning.

## 3. The browser shows security warnings

If "not secure" appears next to the address, or a full-screen warning pops up, the [[SSL certificate|ssl]] has probably expired or been set up incorrectly. Many visitors leave as soon as they see it.

## 4. Nothing has been updated for months

If your [[CMS|cms]] dashboard is piling up update notices for the system, the theme or the [[plugins|plugin]], your website has known flaws that haven't been fixed. They're the most common way in for attacks.

## 5. It looks wrong on a mobile

Elements spilling off the screen, tiny text or buttons you can't tap. If your website isn't [[responsive|responsive]], or a change has knocked it out of place, you lose most of your visitors, who arrive on their mobiles.

## 6. Some content is out of date

Old prices, opening hours that no longer apply, services you no longer offer or last year's date in the footer. As well as confusing customers, they suggest neglect and undermine trust.

## 7. You don't know when the last backup was made

If you can't answer with confidence, the risk is high. Without a recent [[backup|copia-seguridad]], any serious fault could force you to rebuild the website.

## Where to start

If you recognise several of these signs, the recommended order is: first a full backup, then the security updates, then checking the forms and the certificate, and finally speed and content. Doing it in a different order can turn a small problem into a big one.
