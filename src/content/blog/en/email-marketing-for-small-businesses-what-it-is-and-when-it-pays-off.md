Social networks decide who sees your posts; email doesn't. When someone gives you their address and agrees to receive your communications, you have a direct channel to that person. [[Email marketing|email-marketing]] is about making the most of that channel with well-designed campaigns, sent to the right people at the right time.

## Types of send you can use

- **Newsletters**: news, tips or articles from your blog, at a regular frequency.
- **Promotional campaigns**: a seasonal offer, a new product or an event.
- **Automated emails**: a welcome for a new subscriber, a reminder or a greeting, sent on their own when a condition is met thanks to [[automation|automatizacion]].
- **Customer communications**: changes to opening hours, new terms or important notices.

## When it pays off

Email works especially well for businesses customers come back to: a shop, a restaurant, a training centre, a clinic or accommodation with repeat guests. It also works when the buying decision is made slowly and it helps to remind people of your offer from time to time.

Signs it could suit you:

1. You have customers who buy once and you don't know how to bring them back.
2. You launch news or promotions and on social media they reach few people.
3. You collect email addresses (on your website, in the shop, with bookings) and do nothing with them.
4. Your business is seasonal and you want to reactivate customers before each season.

## What you need before sending

- **A permission-based list.** To send marketing communications you generally need each person's prior consent, as required by the [[GDPR|rgpd]] and the rules on electronic marketing communications. Buying databases is not an option.
- **A goal for each campaign**: a booking, a purchase, an article read.
- **A design that reads well on mobile**, with your brand identity and a single clear [[call to action|cta]] button.
- **A destination page that matches** the email, such as a [[landing page|landing-page]], when the campaign leads to a specific offer.
- **Time or help** to prepare content regularly.

## Segmentation is the key

Sending the same thing to everyone is the quickest way to be ignored. Splitting your database by interests, language, customer type or date of last purchase lets you send more relevant messages. In Tenerife, for example, a business with both resident and visiting customers may need different campaigns, in different languages, for each group.

## Measure to improve

Every campaign leaves data behind: how many people open it, how many click and how many unsubscribe. Reviewing it after each send lets you adjust subject lines, content and frequency, and tells you whether email is helping you sell.
