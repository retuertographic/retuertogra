Launching a digital tourist guide is the beginning, not the end. Many guides are published with great enthusiasm and, a few months later, have outdated videos, points that no longer exist and nobody looking after them. These are the most common mistakes in [[geolocation|geolocalizacion]]-based content projects, and how to tell whether your guide is working.

## Common mistakes

**1. Designing for the computer, not the phone.** Visitors use the guide in the street or on a trail. If the texts are long, the buttons small or the map awkward to use with one finger, they'll give up. The design must be [[responsive|responsive]] from the very first sketch.

**2. Videos that are too long.** A ten-minute video may work at home, but not in front of a monument. Short pieces that get to the point work better.

**3. Content in one language only.** In destinations with visitors from many countries, a single-language guide leaves out a large part of the audience.

**4. Inaccurate locations.** A point in the wrong place on the map wastes time and undermines trust. Check every location on the ground.

**5. Forgetting the practical information.** Opening hours, access, parking or nearby services are often what people look up most.

**6. Nobody in charge.** If nobody on the team is responsible for maintaining the content, the guide goes out of date. The admin dashboard makes the job easier, but someone still has to do it.

**7. Not promoting it.** If the guide doesn't appear on your website, your social media or at information points, and the landing page doesn't look after its [[SEO|seo]], it will hardly be used.

## How to measure results

Before launch, decide what you want to achieve: more visits to lesser-known areas? A fuller museum visit? More enquiries for local businesses? From there, choose a few [[KPIs|kpi]] and review them every month. With a web analytics tool you can track, for example:

| Indicator | What it tells you |
| --- | --- |
| Visits to the guide | Whether people find it |
| Most viewed places and routes | What really interests visitors |
| Video plays | Which content works |
| Most used languages | Where translation is worth investing in |
| Clicks on related services | Whether the guide generates local activity |

Agree from the start with whoever builds the guide which data can be measured and how, so you don't find out once it's already live.

Combine the figures with feedback: ask at the tourist office or at the end of an excursion what people thought of the guide. Visitors' comments often point to problems the numbers don't show.

## Regular reviews

Schedule a review at least once a season: remove what no longer exists, update opening hours, replace videos that have aged and add new places. A living guide is a guide that gets used.
