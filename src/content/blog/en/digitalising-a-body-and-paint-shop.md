In many body and paint shops the information about each car is scattered: the job card on paper, the photos on the phone of whoever took the vehicle in, the quote in an email and the customer update in a chat. It works while there are only a few cars, but as soon as volume picks up the usual questions appear: where is this car up to? Who spoke to the customer? Where are the photos of the damage? Digitalising the shop doesn't mean filling it with screens; it means having every repair in one place.

## Signs that paper has reached its limit

- To find out the status of a car, someone has to go and ask in the bay.
- Intake photos get lost, or nobody knows which vehicle they belong to.
- Customers ring several times a day to ask whether their car is ready.
- Approved quotes and completed work don't match what gets invoiced.
- Nobody remembers what was done to a vehicle that comes back months later.

If two or more sound familiar, the problem isn't effort, it's the tool.

## What to digitalise first

You don't need to change everything at once. This order usually works well:

1. **The vehicle and customer record.** Registration, model, contact details and reason for the visit. It's the foundation for everything else.
2. **The repair status.** A few clear stages (received, being quoted, approved, bodywork, paint, ready for collection) are enough for the whole team to speak the same language.
3. **The photos.** Always linked to the vehicle, from intake to handover.
4. **Quotes and completed work.** So you can compare what was approved with what was done.
5. **Deliveries, pick-ups and courtesy vehicles.** Once the rest is working, organising the schedule is much easier.

## How to do it without stopping the shop

- **Start with new cars.** Those already in can finish under the old system.
- **Choose one person to own digital intake** for the first few weeks.
- **Use a phone or tablet on the shop floor.** If recording a photo means walking to the office, nobody will do it. Good [[user experience|ux]] is what decides whether a tool gets used or abandoned.
- **Review at the end of each week** which details are missing and why.

## Generic tool or your own app

A shared spreadsheet can do the job at first, but it doesn't notify customers, doesn't keep photos in order and doesn't show a dashboard with the status of the whole shop. An app designed for the shop, usable on any device thanks to a [[responsive|responsive]] design, brings in the [[automation|automatizacion]] of repetitive tasks and leaves the team focused on repairs.

## What to measure afterwards

Choose two or three simple [[KPIs|kpi]]: average time from intake to handover, number of customer calls asking about their car, and quotes awaiting a reply. If they go down, digitalisation is working.
