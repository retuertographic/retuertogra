Resolving an incident is half the job. The other half is letting people know. When a resident reports a problem and never hears back, even if it has been fixed, the impression is that nobody listened. Publishing the status of incidents and the actions taken changes that perception and, along the way, cuts down on repeat reports and calls asking for updates.

## Why show incidents publicly

- **It avoids duplicates.** If a resident sees that the pothole on their street has already been reported and is in progress, they don't need to report it again.
- **It builds trust.** Seeing resolved incidents shows that the channel actually works.
- **It improves communication** between the council, residents' associations and citizens, who all share the same information.
- **It puts decisions in context.** An action history explains why one area is prioritised over another.

## What to publish and what not to

Transparency doesn't mean publishing everything. A reasonable guideline:

| Publish | Don't publish |
| --- | --- |
| Category and description of the problem | Name and contact details of the person reporting |
| Approximate or exact location of the problem | Photos showing recognisable people or number plates |
| Current status and date of the last action | Internal team comments |
| A short summary of what has been done | Information outside the public remit |

Photos and personal data must be handled in line with [[GDPR|rgpd]]. It's wise to review images before making them public and to explain clearly what will and won't be shown.

## A map is worth more than a list

Thanks to [[geolocation|geolocalizacion]], incidents can be shown on a map of the area, so any resident can see at a glance what's happening in their neighbourhood. Make sure the public view works well on mobile, with a [[responsive|responsive]] design, because that's where most people will open it.

## News as a complement

Besides incidents, publishing relevant information helps you get ahead: planned water cuts, roadworks on a street, changes to waste collection, or warnings about calima or heavy rain, both common in the Canary Islands. A news section within the same app keeps all the information in one place.

## Mistakes to avoid

1. **Publishing without updating.** A map full of incidents marked "received" for months does more harm than having no map at all.
2. **Statuses with no explanation.** If an incident is dismissed, say why.
3. **Bureaucratic language.** Write the way you'd talk to a neighbour.
4. **Forgetting closure.** Every resolved incident should notify the person who reported it.

## How to measure the effect

Before and after opening the public view, compare the number of duplicate incidents and the calls asking about a report's status. They're two simple [[KPIs|kpi]] that show whether transparency is saving work.
