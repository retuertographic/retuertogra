Choosing a [[CRM|crm]] is the easy part. What decides whether the team will use it, or drift back to the spreadsheet within a month, is how it's set up. With a little preparation, the tool adapts to the way you sell rather than the other way round. These are the steps we recommend.

## 1. Take stock of where your information is

Before configuring anything, write down where your customers come from and where you currently keep what you know about them: WhatsApp, email, website forms, a paper diary or spreadsheets. That list tells you which channels to connect first and which data you'll need to import.

## 2. Clean up your contacts before importing them

Importing a messy list just moves the mess. Spend some time on:

- Removing duplicates and contacts that are no longer relevant.
- Using one format for phone numbers and email addresses.
- Deciding the minimum details each profile will hold: name, company, phone, email and where the contact came from.

Remember that customer data is personal data: keep only what you need and handle it in line with [[GDPR|rgpd]].

## 3. Define the stages of your funnel

Your [[sales funnel|embudo]] should reflect how you really sell. For most small businesses, five stages are enough:

| Stage | What it means |
| --- | --- |
| New enquiry | Someone has written in and hasn't had a reply yet |
| In conversation | Their needs are being clarified |
| Quote sent | The customer has a proposal |
| Won | They've accepted |
| Lost | They're not going ahead (note the reason) |

Avoid creating ten stages on day one. You can always add more when you need them.

## 4. Set up users and permissions

Decide who handles each channel and who can see what. A salesperson needs their own opportunities; management needs the overall view. Assigning owners stops two people replying to the same customer, or an enquiry being left without anyone responsible.

## 5. Connect your channels and forms

Sync your email and website forms so each new enquiry lands in the unified inbox on its own and creates or updates the contact's profile. This [[integration|integracion]] is what really saves time, because it removes the copying by hand.

## 6. Create your first follow-up rules

Agree on a few simple guidelines and turn them into tasks and reminders:

1. Every new enquiry gets a reply the same day.
2. Three days after sending a quote, a reminder is scheduled to get back in touch.
3. Every lost opportunity is closed with its reason.

## 7. Train the team and review after two weeks

A short session to cover the basics is usually enough. What matters is reviewing after two weeks: what's being used, what's surplus and what's missing. Adjusting early stops the tool being abandoned.
