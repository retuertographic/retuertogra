An incident reporting app can receive reports with perfect photos and locations, but if nobody inside the organisation knows who should deal with each one, the problem simply moves: from the switchboard to an overflowing inbox. The least visible part, and the most important, is deciding before you start how incidents are classified, who handles them and what each status means.

## Step 1: define a few, well-named categories

Categories are the first thing residents see when they report something, so they must make sense without thinking. Some common ideas for a town:

- Street lighting
- Roads and pavements
- Cleaning and waste
- Parks and gardens
- Street furniture
- Other

Six clear categories are better than twenty overlapping ones. If a resident hesitates between two, they'll pick either and the incident will end up in the wrong place. Look at the reports you already receive by phone or email to see which topics come up again and again.

## Step 2: assign an owner to each category

Each category needs a person or department to receive it. Bear in mind:

- **A main owner and a deputy**, for holidays and sick leave.
- **Who reassigns** a misclassified incident.
- **What happens to those outside your remit**, for example ones that depend on the island council (the Cabildo, in Tenerife) or another authority: it helps to have a ready-made message explaining where to go.

## Step 3: agree what each status means

A status that each person interprets differently confuses both the team and the resident. A simple example:

| Status | What it means |
| --- | --- |
| Received | It has arrived and hasn't been reviewed yet |
| Under review | Someone is assessing it |
| In progress | Action is planned or under way |
| Resolved | The problem has been fixed |
| Dismissed | Not applicable or a duplicate, with an explanation |

Every status change should be logged in the action history, and the resident should be notified.

## Step 4: write template replies

Preparing short texts for frequent cases saves time and keeps the tone consistent: "We've received your report", "We've scheduled the repair", "This incident had already been reported". They don't replace a personal reply when one is needed, but they cover most cases.

## Step 5: review every month

Check which categories collect the most reports, which take longest to resolve and how many incidents are reassigned because they were misclassified. If a category gets lots of reports that don't belong there, its name may not be clear. Those figures are your management [[KPIs|kpi]].

## What technology brings

[[Geolocation|geolocalizacion]] and photos provide context, but what really saves work is having classification, assignment and notifications in the same flow. That [[automation|automatizacion]] avoids forwarding emails between departments. If the organisation already uses other tools, an [[integration|integracion]] can connect the two.
