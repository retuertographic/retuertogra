A [[chatbot|chatbot]] can work perfectly behind the scenes and still scare customers away because of the way it talks. Endless messages, menus with ten options or cold switchboard phrases make people abandon the conversation. The good news is that writing good replies doesn't require any programming: it requires knowing your customers and putting yourself in their shoes.

## Start with what people really ask you

Before you write anything, gather the real questions you receive on WhatsApp over a few weeks. Group them by topic: prices, opening hours, availability, location, the status of an order. That list is the bot's script. If a question comes up often, it deserves a carefully written answer; if it comes up once a month, it may be better for a person to handle it.

## Rules for sounding natural

- **Short messages**: two or three sentences at most. On a mobile, a long block of text doesn't get read.
- **One question per message**. If you need several details, ask for them one at a time.
- **Specific options**: buttons or quick replies with three or four clear choices, not a never-ending numbered menu.
- **Your business's tone**: if your shop is informal and light-hearted, the bot should be too. If you are a professional firm, keep it more formal.
- **Be honest about what it is**: saying that it's an automated assistant and that a person can take over the conversation builds trust.

## Example: before and after

Before: "Welcome. Select an option: 1 Information 2 Prices 3 Opening hours 4 Location 5 Other".

After: "Hi! I'm the shop's assistant. Can I help you with prices, opening hours or an order? If you'd rather talk to the team, just type 'person'".

The second message says the same thing, but it offers a clear way through to a person and sounds like someone who wants to help.

## Common mistakes

1. **Not planning a way out**: if the bot fails to understand something twice in a row, it should pass the conversation to the team instead of repeating the same answer.
2. **Promising what it can't control**: fixed prices or lead times that depend on each case should be given as indicative.
3. **Forgetting about out-of-hours messages**: at night, the bot should say when a person will reply and keep a record of the enquiry.
4. **Not reviewing conversations**: reading a few real chats every week is the best way to spot confusing replies.

## Measure and improve

Notice at what point customers drop out of the conversation and which questions always end up being passed to the team. Those two figures tell you which replies to rewrite and which new topics to add. And if every chat is logged in a [[CRM|crm]], you'll be able to see which ones end in a sale.
