If your business has delivery vans, service vehicles, construction machinery or equipment that moves from place to place, you probably know these calls: "Where are you?", "How long will you be?", "Who's got the machine?". Without visibility of where your vehicles or assets are, planning the work and keeping the service under control becomes hard. A location and tracking system takes care of that.

## What a tracking system is

It's a tool that shows on a map where your vehicles or assets are, using [[geolocation|geolocalizacion]], and keeps a record of the journeys they've made. You check it from the office computer or from your phone, and it usually includes:

- Location display on a map.
- Route history.
- Alerts.
- Users with different permissions and a control panel.
- Reports and data export.

## Signs that you need it

- **You waste time tracking down your team** by phone to assign an urgent job.
- **You don't know which vehicle is closest** to a customer who has just called.
- **Customers ask when the technician will arrive** and you can't give an accurate answer.
- **You struggle to justify hours or journeys** when invoicing a job or reviewing costs.
- **You have machinery or equipment spread** across several sites and don't always know where each item is.
- **You're concerned about out-of-hours use** or the security of parked assets.

If two or three of these sound familiar, it's worth looking into.

## What you can expect

The figures depend on each business, so be wary of generic promises. The usual improvements are organisational: assigning jobs based on real information, giving customers more reliable arrival times, reviewing routes that repeat and having objective data when discussing costs. On an island like Tenerife, with mountain roads, heavy rush-hour traffic and journeys between north and south, knowing the actual routes helps you plan better.

## Questions to ask before deciding

1. **What do you want to track?** Vehicles, machinery, trailers or other equipment.
2. **Who will check the information?** Management, the office, team leaders.
3. **Which alerts would be useful?** Think about the situations you currently find out about too late.
4. **Which reports do you need**, and how often?
5. **Does it need to connect to other tools?** If you need to pass the data to another program, plan it from the start as an [[integration|integracion]].

With these answers written down, any proposal will fit what you need much more closely.

## A note on privacy

When vehicles are driven by your staff, tracking involves processing personal data. Data protection law, starting with the [[GDPR|rgpd]], requires you to inform employees clearly, use the data only for the stated purpose and limit who can access it. Check with your adviser before you start.
