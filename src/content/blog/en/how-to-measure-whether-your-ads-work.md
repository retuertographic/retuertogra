Ad platforms show dozens of figures, and it's easy to get lost among them or cling to the most reassuring one. But a business only cares about one question: does what I spend on advertising bring in more than it costs? To answer it, you only need to look at a few figures, always the same ones, and relate them to your actual sales.

## The figures that matter

| Figure | What it tells you |
| --- | --- |
| Impressions and reach | How many times and to how many people the ad was shown |
| Clicks | How many people were interested enough to click through |
| Conversions | How many did what you wanted: call, book, request a quote, buy |
| Cost per conversion | What each of those results costs you on average |
| Closed sales | How many conversions became paying customers |

The first two figures help you improve the ad; the last three tell you whether it's paying off. Those are your main [[KPIs|kpi]].

## How to work out whether it pays

1. **Add up what you've invested** over the period, including the ad budget and the management and design work.
2. **Count the real customers** who came from the campaign, not just clicks or forms.
3. **Work out what each customer brings in**: the margin on their first purchase or, if they tend to return, what they bring over time.
4. **Compare.** If what those customers bring in exceeds the investment, the [[return on investment|roi]] is positive and it's worth continuing and improving.

For step 2 it really helps to log each contact in a [[CRM|crm]] or at least in a spreadsheet noting where they came from and whether they bought.

## Mistakes that make each customer cost more

- **Not tracking conversions.** Without them, you only know there were clicks, not whether they helped.
- **Sending traffic to the home page.** A specific [[landing page|landing-page]] that talks about the same thing as the ad converts better than a generic page.
- **Targeting too broadly.** Showing the ad to people who can't become customers, for instance outside your service area, spends budget with no chance of a sale.
- **One ad for the whole journey.** Someone discovering you and someone about to decide need different messages within the [[funnel|embudo]].
- **Changing everything every day.** Campaigns need some time to produce reliable data.
- **Replying late.** An enquiry that takes a day to answer may end up with a competitor, however good the ad was.
- **Not refreshing the creatives.** If people always see the same ad, they stop noticing it.

## Review it on a schedule

Check the main figures every week to spot problems, and carry out a fuller review every month, comparing with the previous month. What matters is the trend, not a single day.
