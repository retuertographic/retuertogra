These days anyone can put together a website in an afternoon using a template. So why do some businesses commission custom development? The answer depends on what your website needs to do: an online business card is not the same as an online shop, a portal with a customer area or a site that has to connect with the company's other tools.

## What each option is

**A template website** starts from a ready-made design that's adapted with your text, colours and images. It's usually built on a [[CMS|cms]] and extended with [[plugins|plugin]] to add features.

**A custom website** is designed and developed from your needs: the structure, features and look are decided with your customers and the way you work in mind, not by what a template offers.

## When a template may be enough

- You need a simple presence: who you are, what you do and how to get in touch.
- The content rarely changes and there are no processes behind it (bookings, orders, requests).
- You have time to maintain it and keep the add-ons updated.

## When a custom website pays off

- **Your website sells or brings in customers**, and every second of loading time and every step of a form counts.
- **You need your own features**: private customer areas, admin dashboards, catalogues or shops with specific rules.
- **It has to talk to other tools** through an [[integration|integracion]]: your management software, the CRM or external services.
- **The template is already holding you back**: plugins are piling up, the site is slow or every change breaks something else.
- **You want it to look good and work just as well on mobile**, which is where a large share of visits comes from.

## Quick comparison

| Aspect | Template | Custom |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Getting started | Quick | Needs more planning |
| Design | Adapted from an existing one | Your own, in line with your brand |
| Features | Whatever the plugins offer | Whatever your business needs |
| Speed and technical [[SEO|seo]] | Depends on the template | Optimised from the start |
| Growth | Limited by the template | Grows with the project |

## Questions to help you decide

1. What does the website need to achieve: inform, sell, receive requests?
2. Which tools do you already use, and which should it connect with?
3. Who will update it, and how often?
4. Is your current website losing you customers because it's slow, outdated or hard to update?

If the answers point to processes, integrations or growth, a custom website is usually the more sensible investment in the medium term.
