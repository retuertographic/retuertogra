Setting up a tracking system doesn't take long; getting the team to use it properly and making the data useful takes a little more planning. These steps will help you do it in an orderly way, whether you have five vans or several machines spread across different sites.

## 1. Decide what you want it for

Write your main goal in one sentence: assigning jobs to the nearest vehicle, knowing where the machinery is, giving customers reliable arrival times or reviewing travel costs. That goal will determine which alerts, reports and users you need. Without it, it's easy to end up with a map full of dots that nobody looks at.

## 2. Take an inventory

Make a list of everything you want to track: registration or identifier, type of vehicle or asset, which team it belongs to and who usually uses it. Give them clear names ("Van 3 – North maintenance") so they're recognisable at a glance on the map.

## 3. Decide who sees what

Not everyone needs the same information. One possible split:

| Profile | What they check |
| --- | --- |
| Management | Reports and the overall picture |
| Office or dispatch | The location map to assign jobs |
| Team leaders | Only their own team's vehicles |

User management lets you fine-tune this access, and limiting who sees what is also good data protection practice.

## 4. Inform your staff

This step is as important as the technical one. Explain to the people who drive the vehicles what will be tracked, for what purpose, during which hours and who will see the data. Data protection law ([[GDPR|rgpd]]) requires this transparency, and it also avoids misunderstandings: presented as a tool for organising work better rather than as surveillance, it's far better accepted. If in doubt, seek employment or legal advice.

## 5. Start with a few alerts

Alerts are useful, but too many end up being ignored. Start with two or three linked to your goal and add more when you see they're needed.

## 6. Pilot with a small group

Start with part of the fleet for a few weeks. Check that locations can be viewed properly on both computer and phone, that alerts reach the right people and that the reports answer your questions.

## 7. Connect your other tools if needed

If you want to use the data in another program, data export is the first step. For automatic connections with other systems, for example via an [[API|api]], raise it when you ask for a proposal, as the exact features are defined case by case.

## 8. Review after a month

After the first month, sit down with the people who use it every day: what works, what's unnecessary and what's missing. Adjust permissions, alerts and reports. From then on, set a few [[KPIs|kpi]] to track improvement with data.

## Start-up checklist

1. Goal written in one sentence.
2. Inventory of vehicles and assets with clear names.
3. User profiles and permissions.
4. Staff informed.
5. Two or three initial alerts.
6. Pilot and one-month review.
