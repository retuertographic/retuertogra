A digital tourist guide is only as good as what's in it. The technology provides the map and the [[geolocation|geolocalizacion]], but the places, videos and texts need to be prepared carefully. Here's an organised way to do it, designed for small teams at a council, a natural site or an excursion company.

## 1. Choose the places and group them

Start with a list of every point you'd like to include and keep only the ones that genuinely add something for the visitor. Then group them:

- **By route**: walking, driving or themed routes (viewpoints, heritage, food).
- **By type**: beaches, museums, services, car parks or information points.

For each place, note its exact location, how to get there and whether it has opening hours or access restrictions. Twenty well-prepared points are better than a hundred empty profiles.

## 2. Film short videos designed for mobile

Visitors will watch the videos standing up, often in the sun and with little time. A few guidelines:

- Keep them short: just enough to tell the essentials about the place.
- Make the site clearly recognisable in the first few seconds.
- Add subtitles, as many people watch videos with the sound off.
- Get clean sound if there's a voiceover: wind on the coast or in the mountains is very noticeable.

Also film general shots of the area that can be used for the guide's home screen or for social media.

## 3. Write useful profiles

Each profile should answer what someone standing there wants to know: what it is, why it's worth it, how long the visit takes and what's nearby. Short sentences and practical details rather than long history lessons.

## 4. Prepare the languages

In the Canary Islands, many visitors speak English or German, among other languages. Decide from the outset which languages you'll offer and translate both the profiles and the subtitles. A translation reviewed by a person inspires more confidence than an unchecked machine translation.

## 5. Check on the ground

Before publishing, walk at least one route with your phone in hand: check that the points are correctly placed on the map, that the videos load with the local signal and that the directions make sense. It's the most reliable [[user experience|ux]] test there is.

## 6. Publish and promote the guide

A guide nobody can find is no use. Link to it from your website and social media, and consider signs with a [[QR code|codigo-qr]] at tourist offices, car parks or trailheads. Look after the [[SEO|seo]] of the page that leads to it too, so it appears when someone searches for things to see in your area.

## Quick summary

1. List of places with location and access.
2. Short videos with subtitles.
3. Profiles with practical information.
4. Reviewed translations.
5. On-the-ground test.
6. Promotion plan.
