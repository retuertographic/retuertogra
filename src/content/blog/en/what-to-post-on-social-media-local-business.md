Opening a social media account is easy. The hard part is knowing what to say week after week without repeating yourself or posting just for the sake of it. The good news is that a local business has more material than it thinks: its team, the way it works, its customers and the place where it is. You just need to learn to see it as content.

## Ideas that work for almost any business

- **Your product or service up close.** What it is, how it's used, who it's for. A well-taken photo and two clear sentences are worth more than a long text.
- **The process.** How a dish is prepared, how a part is repaired, how an order is put together. Showing the work builds trust.
- **The team.** Who serves, who cooks, who is behind the counter. Familiar faces make customers feel at home.
- **Answers to frequent questions.** If people often ask you the same thing, turn it into a post.
- **Customer reviews**, always with their permission and without editing what they said.
- **News and seasons.** Changes to opening hours, new products, seasonal menus or special dates.
- **Your surroundings.** Whether you're in La Laguna, the south of Tenerife or a particular neighbourhood of Santa Cruz, sharing what's nearby helps people searching in the area.

## Adapt each piece to the network

The same topic can work on several platforms, but not in the same way:

| Network | What usually fits best |
| --- | --- |
| Instagram | Polished photos, carousels and short videos |
| Facebook | News, events and the local community |
| TikTok | Spontaneous vertical videos and processes |
| YouTube | Longer explainer videos |
| LinkedIn | Company, team and business-to-business services |
| Google Business Profile | Offers, news and photos of your premises |

You don't need to be everywhere. Choose the networks your customers use and do them well. And don't forget your [Google Business Profile](post:seo-perfil-google): for many local businesses it's the first place people see them.

## How to write the copy

1. **Start with what matters.** The first line should say what the post is about.
2. **Talk the way you talk to customers.** Neither too formal nor full of jargon.
3. **Include a specific action**: book, send a message, drop by the shop or visit the website. That [[call to action|cta]] is what turns a post into an opportunity.
4. **Use the words people search with.** Naming the service and the area helps people find you, just like a good [[keyword|palabra-clave]] on your website.

## Look after the visuals

Posts should look like they come from the same brand: the same colours, typefaces and photo style. If you have [[brand guidelines|manual-marca]], apply them on social media too. Consistency makes people recognise you before they read your name, and strengthens your [[branding|branding]].
