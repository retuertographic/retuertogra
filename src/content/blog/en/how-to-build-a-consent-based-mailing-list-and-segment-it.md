A small mailing list of people who want to hear from you is worth far more than thousands of addresses that never asked for your messages. Building it properly from the start avoids legal problems, improves email delivery and makes your [[email marketing|email-marketing]] campaigns work.

## 1. Give people a reason to subscribe

Nobody hands over their email address for nothing. Explain what they'll receive and how often: monthly news, subscriber offers, useful tips or early access to events. Some places to ask:

- A form on your website or on a [[landing page|landing-page]].
- The checkout or booking process.
- Your shop counter, with a paper form or a tablet.
- Your social media, linking to the form.

## 2. Ask for consent properly

Under the [[GDPR|rgpd]], consent must be freely given, specific, informed and unambiguous. In practice:

- **Unticked box** by default. A pre-ticked box doesn't count as consent.
- **Clear wording** about what you'll send, who the controller is and where to read the privacy policy.
- **Separate from other terms**: accepting the terms of sale isn't the same as agreeing to receive marketing.
- **Records**: keep a note of when and how each person subscribed, because you must be able to prove it.

Using double opt-in is highly recommended: after signing up, the person receives an email to confirm their address. This avoids mistyped addresses and sign-ups made by someone else.

In some cases the rules allow you to email existing customers about products or services similar to those they bought, provided they were given the chance to object when their details were collected and in every message. As the exact requirements depend on the situation, check with your adviser before relying on this.

## 3. Make unsubscribing easy

Every marketing email must include a simple, free way to unsubscribe. Processing unsubscribes promptly isn't just an obligation: a list of interested people simply works better.

## 4. Ask only for the data you need

To start with, the email address and perhaps the name and language are enough. Other details, such as interests, can be asked for later. Asking for less increases sign-ups and simplifies data protection.

## 5. Segment the database

Some common ways to segment:

| Segment | Example use |
| --- | --- |
| Language | Campaigns in Spanish, English or German |
| Contact type | Customers versus subscribers who haven't bought yet |
| Interests | Product categories or specific services |
| Activity | Recent purchase or inactive for months |

If you use a [[CRM|crm]], connecting it to your email tool helps keep segments up to date without manual work.

## 6. Keep the list clean

Remove addresses that bounce and, every so often, try to re-engage people who haven't opened your emails for a long time; if they don't respond, consider removing them. A clean list improves delivery and means your statistics reflect reality.
