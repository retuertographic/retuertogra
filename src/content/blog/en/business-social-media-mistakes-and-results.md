Many businesses feel social media takes up their time without giving anything back. Sometimes that's true, but it's almost always down to a handful of recurring mistakes and not measuring what matters. Fixing them doesn't take more hours, just a bit more order.

## Common mistakes

- **Posting without a goal.** If you don't know what you expect from a post (an enquiry, a booking, a visit), you can't tell whether it worked.
- **Being on every network at once.** Opening five profiles and keeping them half-maintained looks worse than looking after two properly.
- **Disappearing for weeks.** An account whose last post is months old suggests the business has stalled.
- **Only talking about yourself.** If everything is an offer, people stop paying attention. Mix useful, personal and commercial content.
- **Careless or inconsistent visuals.** Dark photos, different colours on every piece or copy full of typos erode trust. Your [[visual identity|identidad-visual]] should show here too.
- **Not replying.** Unanswered comments and messages are lost opportunities, and everyone else can see them.
- **Not saying what to do next.** A post without a [[call to action|cta]] leaves an interested customer unsure how to take the next step.
- **Only looking at likes.** They're nice, but they don't pay the bills.

## What really to measure

Choose a few indicators and always review them the same way, once a month:

| Indicator | What it tells you |
| --- | --- |
| Reach | How many people your content reached |
| Engagement | Whether what you post interests people: comments, saves, shares |
| Link or website clicks | Whether social media sends people to your website or [[landing page|landing-page]] |
| Messages and calls | Whether social media generates real enquiries |
| Attributable bookings or sales | Whether enquiries turn into business |

The first indicators help you improve the content; the last ones tell you whether it's worth it. With these [[KPIs|kpi]] you can discuss the [[return|roi]] on your social media with data rather than impressions.

## How to tell where each customer comes from

1. **Ask.** "How did you hear about us?" is still the simplest tool. Write the answer down.
2. **Use different links** for each network, so your website statistics show which visits come from where.
3. **Create offers or codes specific** to social media, so you know how many sales come from there.
4. **Compare month on month**, not day by day. Isolated figures mislead; trends don't.

## What to do with the data

Look at which topics and formats generated the most engagement and enquiries, and repeat them in the next calendar. Whatever still doesn't work after several attempts, change it or drop it. Social media improves through an accumulation of small decisions, not one miracle post.
