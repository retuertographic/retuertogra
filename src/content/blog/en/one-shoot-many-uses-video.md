A common mistake is thinking of a shoot as "the company video": a single piece that goes up on the website and is forgotten. With a little planning, the same filming day can provide material for your website, for months of social media and for several campaigns. The key is to film with all those destinations in mind from the start.

## What you can get from one day

- **A main introduction video**, one or two minutes long, for the website.
- **Several short clips**, each with a single idea: a product, a process, an answer from the interview.
- **Pieces for ads**, with a direct message and an ending that invites action.
- **Stills and images** for posts, cover photos or campaigns.
- **B-roll**: shots of the premises, the team at work or the surroundings, useful for future pieces.

## Each channel needs its own format

| Destination | Usual format | What to watch |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Website | Landscape | Fast loading and understandable without sound |
| YouTube | Landscape | Clear title and description |
| Stories, reels and TikTok | Vertical | Keep what matters in the centre of the frame |
| Feed posts | Square or vertical | One message per piece |
| Ads | Several at once | Versions for each placement |

That's why it's worth saying so before filming: a shot framed only for landscape sometimes can't be cropped to vertical without cutting off the person speaking.

## Adding video to your website without slowing it down

1. **Don't upload the original file** as it is. It should be compressed and prepared for the web.
2. **Check the loading speed.** A heavy video on the home page can worsen your [[Core Web Vitals|core-web-vitals]] and make the page slow to appear.
3. **Check how it looks on mobile.** The site should be [[responsive|responsive]], and so should the video.
4. **Add text alongside it.** A video doesn't replace written content: explain the essentials on the page too.
5. **Place it where it helps people decide**, for example on a service [[landing page|landing-page]], near the contact button.

## Subtitles and on-screen text

Many people watch videos on their phones in places where they can't turn the sound on. Subtitles and on-screen text make sure the message still gets through. If you work in tourism, consider versions in other languages.

## Always end with an action

Every piece should make clear what to do next: book, call, send a message or visit the website. That [[call to action|cta]] is the difference between a nice video and a useful one.
