Every week, supplier invoices arrive by email, paper delivery notes come in with the goods and photos are taken on a phone in the stockroom. Someone has to open them one by one and copy the details into a spreadsheet or the management software. It's slow, thankless work, and a single mistyped figure can throw out the accounts or the stock count. [[Automating|automatizacion]] invoices and delivery notes with AI takes care of exactly that task.

## What it actually does

The system receives the document, reads it and extracts the details you need, just as a person would, but in seconds. Specifically, it:

- **Reads documents in different formats**: PDF, photo or scanned paper.
- **Extracts the key details**: supplier, dates, amounts, taxes and line items.
- **Classifies and validates** each document, telling an invoice apart from a delivery note, for example.
- **Exports the information** to spreadsheets, Excel or CSV.
- **Sends it to other systems** via [[webhook|webhook]] to your [[CRM|crm]], ERP or management software.
- **Alerts you** when a document needs a human check.

That last point matters: the idea isn't to replace anyone's judgement but to take away the mechanical part. People review the doubtful cases instead of typing everything in.

## When it pays off

Not every business needs to automate its documents. It usually makes sense when several of these apply:

1. **You receive a lot of supplier documents** every week or month.
2. **Someone spends set hours** typing them up.
3. **Typing errors have consequences**: accounts that don't balance, stock that doesn't match or duplicate payments.
4. **The data has to end up in several places**: a control sheet, the management software and your accountant.
5. **Volumes grow** in high season, as happens in many hospitality and retail businesses in the Canary Islands.

If you only receive a handful of invoices a month, it's probably not worth it. If there are dozens or hundreds, the time saved soon shows.

## Who it's for

It's a particularly good fit for companies, accounting firms and shops that receive many supplier documents: a restaurant getting delivery notes from several distributors every day, a shop with frequent restocking or an accounting firm processing paperwork for many clients.
