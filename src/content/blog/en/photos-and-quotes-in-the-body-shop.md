In a body and paint shop, documentation matters almost as much as the repair itself. A good photo at intake avoids arguments about a scratch that "was already there", a clear quote speeds up approval and a complete history lets you look after returning customers better. The trouble is that when everything depends on each person's phone and loose sheets of paper, that information disappears just when you need it most.

## Photos: what, when and how

It's worth photographing the vehicle at three points:

1. **At intake.** All four sides of the car, close-ups of the damage, the odometer and any existing details (kerbed wheels, chipped glass).
2. **During the repair.** Removed parts or hidden damage that appears once panels come off. They are the best justification if the quote needs extending.
3. **At handover.** The final result, in the same light and from the same angles as at intake.

A few simple tips:

- Always take the photos in the same order, so nobody forgets a side.
- Look for even light; reflections hide dents.
- Link each photo to the vehicle straight away, not at the end of the day.

## The quote: clear and comparable

A good quote can be understood without phoning the shop. Check that it includes:

- Vehicle and customer details.
- A description of each job, separating bodywork, paint and parts.
- Reference photos of the damage.
- Estimated time frame and conditions.

Once the quote is approved, ideally it stays linked to the repair. That way, when the job is finished, you can compare what was approved with the work completed and spot any difference in time.

## The history: the shop's memory

Every closed repair should add to the vehicle's history: what was done, when, with which photos and with which quote. That history is useful if the customer comes back, if a question arises about an earlier repair or if a different person is handling the customer.

## Common mistakes

- **Photos on the personal phone** of whoever took the car in. If that person isn't around, neither are the photos.
- **Paper quotes with no copy.** They get lost or stained in the bay.
- **Verbal approvals.** Without a record, any extension of the work turns into an argument.
- **Not protecting data.** Records include customers' personal data and must be handled in line with [[GDPR|rgpd]], with access only for those who need it.
