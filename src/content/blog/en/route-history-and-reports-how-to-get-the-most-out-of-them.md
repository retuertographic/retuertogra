Having your fleet on a map is useful from day one. The real value comes over time, as the route history and reports build up data that reveals patterns. Many businesses introduce a tracking system and only ever look at where each vehicle is right now; these ideas will help you go a step further.

## What questions the data can answer

- **Which routes repeat every week?** If several vehicles visit the same area on different days, perhaps visits can be grouped.
- **How much time does each team spend travelling** compared with time spent working for the customer?
- **Which assets sit idle** for too long, and which are overused?
- **What time did someone actually arrive at a job?** Useful when there's a complaint or when justifying hours.
- **Is there any use outside working hours** or outside the expected areas?

## How to organise the review

1. **Appoint someone responsible** for reviewing the reports at a fixed interval, for example weekly.
2. **Set a few indicators.** Three or four [[KPIs|kpi]] are enough: journeys per job, average arrival time, hours of use per asset or number of alerts.
3. **Compare periods**: this month against last, high season against low. In the Canary Islands' tourist areas, activity and traffic vary considerably throughout the year.
4. **Share the conclusions with the team.** If drivers see that the data helps organise routes better, they'll be more cooperative.

## Export and cross-reference the data

Data export lets you take the information into a spreadsheet or other management tools. Cross-referencing it with invoicing or the job calendar helps you work out the real cost of each job and estimate the [[return on investment|roi]] of the system itself. If you need that connection to be automatic, plan it as an [[integration|integracion]].

## Common mistakes

- **Collecting data without looking at it.** A report nobody reads adds nothing.
- **Too many alerts.** If they go off constantly, the team stops paying attention.
- **Using the data for something other than what was communicated.** If you told staff about a particular purpose, stick to it; changing it without notice breeds mistrust and may conflict with data protection law such as the [[GDPR|rgpd]].
- **Keeping data longer than necessary.** Decide how long you keep the history, and why.

## From data to decisions

Reports don't decide for you, but they make visible things you could previously only guess at: a route that can be reorganised, a machine that should be moved to another site or a customer who can be given a more precise time slot. These are small adjustments that, repeated week after week, justify the system.
