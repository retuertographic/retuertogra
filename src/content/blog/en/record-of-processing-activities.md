Of all the documents data protection rules call for, the record of processing activities is the one that best sums up how your business works with personal data. In practice, it's an inventory: what data you hold, about whom, what you use it for, who you share it with and how you protect it. If the supervisory authority ever asks, it's one of the first documents it will want to see.

## Do I have to keep one?

The [[GDPR|rgpd]] provides an exemption for organisations with fewer than 250 employees, but it's very narrow: it doesn't apply if the processing is not occasional, if it's likely to pose a risk to people or if it includes specially protected data, such as health data. Handling customers or payroll on a regular basis isn't occasional, so in practice most shops, practices and small businesses should have one.

## What it must include

For each processing activity (for example "customers", "employees", "bookings" or "newsletter"), the record sets out:

- **Who the controller is** and their contact details.
- **The purpose**: what the data is used for.
- **The categories of people and data**: customers, employees, contacts; name, phone number, address, bank details.
- **Who it's disclosed to**: accountant, banks, public authorities or suppliers.
- **Whether there are transfers outside the European Economic Area**, which is common with some cloud tools.
- **Retention periods**, where they can be set.
- **A general description of the security measures.**

## How to prepare it step by step

1. **List the situations in which you collect data**: sales, bookings, website forms, WhatsApp, CVs, payroll, CCTV cameras.
2. **Group them by purpose.** Each group becomes an activity in the record.
3. **Note where the data is stored**: the [[CRM|crm]], invoicing software, a spreadsheet, the [[email marketing|email-marketing]] tool or the website server.
4. **Identify the suppliers that access it** and check you have a processor agreement with each one.
5. **Decide how long you keep each type of data** and what happens when that period ends.
6. **Describe the security measures**: password-protected access, individual permissions, [[backups|copia-seguridad]], encryption or up-to-date devices.

## A simple example

| Activity | Purpose | Data | Disclosed to |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Customers | Providing the service and invoicing | Name, contact details, tax details | Accountant, bank |
| Employees | HR and payroll | Identification, bank details, contract | Accountant, Social Security |
| Newsletter | Sending news to people who ask for it | Name and email | Mailing tool |

## Keep it alive

A record made once and forgotten in a drawer stops being useful as soon as you change supplier or open a new channel. Review it at least once a year and whenever you bring in a tool or service that handles data.
