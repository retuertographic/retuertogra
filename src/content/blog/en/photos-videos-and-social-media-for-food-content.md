In food, the image is almost everything. A good photo of a dish makes someone save the recipe, ask for information or book. That's why food projects build up hundreds of photos and videos: the finished dish, the step-by-step, the product, the team, Saturday's event. The problem isn't taking them, but finding them when you need them and publishing them without repeating work on the website and on every social network.

## Organise the archive before publishing

1. **One place for everything.** Not each person's phone, not the team chat. Photos and videos should live alongside the content they belong to.
2. **Linked to their recipe, product or service.** That way, when you open a card, you see all its visual material.
3. **Names that mean something.** "green-mojo-step-3.jpg" can be found; "IMG_4821.jpg" can't.
4. **One main photo per card**, with the same framing and proportions throughout, so the whole set looks consistent.
5. **Lightweight versions for the web.** Heavy photos slow the page down and hurt your [[Core Web Vitals|core-web-vitals]].

## Visual consistency

Photos from the same project should look like they belong to one family: similar light, similar backgrounds, the same colour treatment. That consistency is as much part of your [[visual identity|identidad-visual]] as your logo. A simple guideline, even one page long, stops everyone photographing in their own way.

## Website and social media: same content, different format

Social media is the shop window, but the website is where the full information lives. A good way of working:

- **Publish the full card first** (recipe, product or service) on your website.
- **Adapt the material for each network**: a short vertical video, a carousel of steps or the main photo.
- **Always link to the card**, so anyone interested finds the ingredients, steps or contact form.
- **Avoid copying and pasting by hand** everywhere: an [[integration|integracion]] with social media or external services saves steps and reduces mistakes.

## Mistakes to avoid

- Posting a recipe on social media that doesn't exist on the website, or vice versa.
- Using stock photos that look nothing like what you serve.
- Uploading videos without captions: many people watch with the sound off.
- Forgetting image rights when customers or staff appear.

## Measure what works

You don't need a complex report. Each month, check which recipes or products get the most visits on the website, which generate requests through the form and which social posts bring the most people to the site. With those [[KPIs|kpi]] you'll know which kind of content deserves more time and which you can drop.
