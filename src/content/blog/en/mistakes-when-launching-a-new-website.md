Publishing your new website feels like crossing the finish line, but it's really the starting point. Many problems appear in the first few weeks: forms that never arrive, pages Google can no longer find or a mobile version nobody checked carefully. The good news is that almost all of them can be prevented.

## Common launch mistakes

- **Not testing the forms properly.** Send a test request from every form and check that it reaches the right inbox and doesn't end up in spam.
- **Forgetting redirects.** If the old website's addresses stop existing, people arriving from Google or saved links hit an error page.
- **Only checking it on a computer.** A large share of visits comes from mobile. The site has to be genuinely [[responsive|responsive]]: menus, buttons, tables and forms that are easy to use with a thumb.
- **Going live without an [[SSL|ssl]] certificate or with security warnings.** The browser shows it, and it puts visitors off.
- **Leaving placeholder text or empty pages.** A "coming soon" or dummy text undermines credibility.
- **Not setting up measurement tools.** If you don't measure from day one, you'll have nothing to compare against.
- **Assuming it's finished.** A website needs updates, reviews and improvements as it's used.

## Launch-day checklist

1. Test every form and contact button, including the WhatsApp or phone link.
2. Check the website on at least one Android phone, an iPhone and a computer.
3. Confirm that the most-visited old addresses redirect correctly.
4. Make sure the padlock appears on every page.
5. Check legal notices, contact details and opening hours.
6. Switch on visitor measurement and submit the sitemap to Google.

## How to tell whether the website works

More visits doesn't always mean better results. Choose a few [[KPIs|kpi]] tied to the website's goal and review them monthly:

| Indicator | What it tells you |
| --- | --- |
| Requests, bookings or sales | Whether the site meets its main goal |
| Visits from search engines | Whether technical [[SEO|seo]] and content are working |
| Speed and [[Core Web Vitals|core-web-vitals]] | Whether loading is a good experience, especially on mobile |
| Most-visited pages and exits | What interests people and where they drop off |

Compare each month with the previous one and, after a while, with the same period last year: in tourist areas such as Tenerife, the season has a big effect on the figures.

## After launch

Schedule a review after two weeks and another after three months. With the data in hand, decide which pages to improve, what content to add and which features to bring in during the next phase.
