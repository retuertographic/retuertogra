In many small businesses, sales information lives in three places at once: WhatsApp chats, the email inbox and a spreadsheet that someone updates when they have a moment. While volumes are low, it works. But once more enquiries come in or someone else joins the team, the unanswered questions start: who spoke to this customer? Did we send the quote? Did we say we'd call them this week?

## What a chat CRM is

A [[CRM|crm]] is a tool for managing your relationship with customers. A chat CRM focuses on conversations: it gathers the messages arriving through different channels into one inbox and links them to each contact's profile. That way every customer has their full history in one place, instead of depending on one person's phone or email account.

In practice, it does four things:

- **Shows every conversation** in a unified inbox, rather than scattered across phones and email accounts.
- **Tells you where each customer stands**, thanks to an [[opportunity pipeline|embudo]] with clear stages.
- **Makes sure no follow-up is forgotten**, with tasks and reminders assigned to each person.
- **Supports teamwork** with users and permissions, so everyone sees what they need.

## Signs that the time has come

Not every business needs one from day one. These signs suggest you're already crying out for it:

1. **You lose the thread**: a customer writes to you and you have to search several chats and emails to remember what you told them.
2. **More than one person is handling enquiries**, and sometimes two people reply to the same customer, or nobody does.
3. **Quotes are left hanging** because nobody remembers to get back in touch.
4. **The spreadsheet is always out of date**, because copying data by hand is the last job of the day.
5. **You can't answer basic questions**: how many enquiries came in this month or how many ended in a sale.

If you recognise two or more of these, getting your information in order will save you time and sales.

## How it differs from a spreadsheet

A spreadsheet stores data, but it doesn't work for you. It doesn't remind you that it's time to call a customer, it doesn't collect messages on its own and it doesn't record the history of each conversation. A chat CRM syncs with your email and your forms, so information comes in without being copied, and the team always sees the current version.

## Who it makes most sense for

It's especially useful for sales and customer service teams that receive enquiries every day: an estate agency in the south of Tenerife dealing with buyers on WhatsApp and by email, a training academy handling enrolments or a service company with several technicians. In every case the goal is the same: no opportunity should depend on somebody's memory.

## How to start without overcomplicating it

You don't need to move everything over at once. Start with the channels that bring you the most enquiries, define a few sales stages and ask the team to log every new conversation. Within a few weeks you'll have an accurate picture of your sales activity.
