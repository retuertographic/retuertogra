Many businesses run on a mix of spreadsheets, messaging groups, paper forms and some generic tool that never quite fits. While volumes are small, it holds together. But there comes a point when the team spends more time copying data and hunting for information than actually working. That's when a custom application is worth considering.

## Signs that off-the-shelf tools no longer fit

- **The same data is typed in several times** in different places: a spreadsheet, email, the invoicing software.
- **Nobody knows where a request stands** without asking the person handling it.
- **You work with workarounds**: extra columns, duplicated templates or steps you "have to remember to do".
- **Each role needs to see different things** (customers, staff, management) and your current tool doesn't allow it.
- **Work happens away from the office** and information arrives late or incomplete.

If you recognise two or more of these, an application designed around your process will probably save you time and mistakes.

## What a custom application can solve

A custom application is built around the way you work, not the other way round. Some examples of what it typically includes:

- **Dashboards** for customers, employees or administrators, each with the information they need.
- **User, permission and profile management**, so that each person sees and edits only what concerns them.
- **Digital forms, notifications and request tracking**, instead of paperwork and phone calls.
- **[[Geolocation|geolocalizacion]]**, useful when work is done out in the field or at different points around the island.
- **[[Integration|integracion]] with databases and external services**, so information isn't duplicated.

## When you don't need one

Not everything calls for bespoke development. If a tool on the market covers your process without changing how you work, it's usually the sensible choice. A custom application makes sense when the process is your own, it's repeated often and the cost of carrying on with workarounds (lost hours, errors, customers left waiting) is higher than the cost of solving it properly.

## Questions to ask yourself first

1. **Which specific process do I want to improve?** The narrower the scope, the better the project starts.
2. **Who will use the application** and on which device: computer, mobile or both?
3. **Which systems does it need to talk to?** Invoicing, [[CRM|crm]], your website or external services through an [[API|api]].
4. **How will I know it has worked?** Set one or two indicators from the start, such as time per request or errors detected.

With these answers, your conversation with whoever builds the application will be far more productive, and the quote much closer to what you really need.
