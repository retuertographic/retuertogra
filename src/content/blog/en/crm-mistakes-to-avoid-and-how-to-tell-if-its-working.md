A [[CRM|crm]] doesn't improve sales just by being installed. What makes the difference is everyday use: keeping information up to date, actually doing the follow-ups and having someone look at the figures now and then. These are the mistakes we see most often in small businesses, and how to check, without overcomplicating things, whether the tool is doing its job.

## Common mistakes

### Only half using it

If part of the team logs conversations while the rest keep replying from their own phones, the CRM will never have the full picture. The rule needs to be clear: every enquiry goes through the unified inbox.

### An overcomplicated funnel

Ten stages with similar names mean each person interprets them in their own way. A [[funnel|embudo]] with five well-defined stages is more useful than a perfect one nobody understands.

### Not closing lost opportunities

It's tempting to leave quotes that don't come off hanging. But if they aren't marked as lost, the funnel looks fuller than it is and you'll never know why sales slip away.

### Empty profiles

A contact with just a name and a phone number doesn't tell you much. The conversation history, where the contact came from and notes from each call are what let you pick up the relationship months later.

### Ignored reminders

Tasks and reminders only work if someone checks them. Set aside a few minutes at the start of the day to see which follow-ups are due.

### Giving everyone access to everything

Permissions aren't red tape: they protect your customers' personal data, as [[GDPR|rgpd]] requires, and stop someone accidentally changing an opportunity that isn't theirs.

## How to tell if it's working

You don't need a complex dashboard. Pick three or four [[KPIs|kpi]] and review them every month:

| Indicator | What it tells you |
| --- | --- |
| Enquiries received per channel | Where your customers come from and which channel deserves more attention |
| Time to first reply | Whether the team responds quickly |
| Quotes sent and accepted | How many proposals end in a sale |
| Reasons for losing | Whether you lose sales on price, lead times or something else |

What matters isn't one month's figure but the trend. Compare one quarter with the previous one and you'll see whether your changes are having an effect.

## A 20-minute monthly review

1. Open the funnel and close any opportunities that haven't moved for weeks.
2. Review the indicators and compare them with the previous month.
3. Discuss with the team what has worked and what hasn't.
4. Adjust just one thing: a stage, a follow-up rule or a message.

With this routine, the CRM stops being a database and becomes a tool for making decisions.
