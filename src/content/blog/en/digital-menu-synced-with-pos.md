It's a familiar scene in hospitality: a dish's price is changed in the [[POS|tpv]], but the menu on the website still shows the old one, and the [[digital menu|carta-digital]] behind the QR code shows yet another. The customer orders what they saw and the bill shows a different figure. Besides the awkward moment, it's a matter of trust and, in some cases, of complying with the obligation to show the final price.

## The problem: the same information in three places

Most restaurants keep their menu in several places at once: the POS they charge with, the website and the menu guests open at the table. Every seasonal change, every new dish and every sold-out item means updating all three by hand. Sooner or later, one falls behind.

## The solution: a single source

Syncing means the POS becomes the reference and everything else is updated from it through an [[integration|integracion]]. You change the price once and it reaches the website and the digital menu. The same goes for names, descriptions, categories and photos.

## The benefits

- **Fewer mistakes** between what guests see and what they pay.
- **Less time** spent updating menus.
- **Sold-out dishes disappear from the menu** as soon as they are marked in the POS.
- **Menus in several languages** with the same structure, very useful in tourist areas such as Tenerife.
- **Dynamic [[QR codes|codigo-qr]]**: the code printed on the table stays the same even when the menu changes.

## What you need to get started

1. **Know which POS you use** and whether it can export products or connect through an [[API|api]]. Most current systems allow it in some way.
2. **Tidy up the menu**: clear categories, reviewed names and descriptions, allergens listed.
3. **Good photos** of the main dishes. A menu with well-shot images helps guests decide and sells better.
4. **Decide where it will appear**: website, QR menu, screens or ordering platforms.

## Beyond the menu

Once the data is connected, other options open up: bringing the catalogue into your [[CRM|crm]] or management system, analysing which dishes sell best or preparing seasonal promotions without rebuilding the menu each time.
