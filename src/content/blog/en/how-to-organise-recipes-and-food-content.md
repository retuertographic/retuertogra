A food project produces content non-stop: new recipes, seasonal dishes, products, workshops, photos of every dish and videos for social media. At first it all fits in one folder and a couple of documents. But there comes a point when nobody can find the right version of a recipe, the photos are spread across three phones and the website shows dishes you no longer make. Organising that content isn't about looks: it's what lets you publish more, and better, with the same effort.

## Signs you need a structure

- You have several versions of the same recipe and don't know which is current.
- Publishing anything on the website depends on one person.
- Photos and videos aren't linked to the dish or product they belong to.
- Ingredients are written differently in each recipe, so it's impossible to search for "everything with gofio".
- People ask for information by message and you have no form to collect it.

## Step by step to get organised

1. **Take stock.** List what you publish: recipes, products, services (catering, classes, events), news. Each type of content will have its own card.
2. **Define the categories.** For example: starters, mains, desserts; or by product, season or origin. Fewer and clearer is better.
3. **Standardise ingredients.** One name per ingredient, always. That way you can filter and connect recipes.
4. **Decide which fields each card has**: title, description, ingredients, steps, times, photos, video and category. We cover this in detail in another article.
5. **Centralise photos and videos.** Each file linked to its recipe or product, with a name that identifies it.
6. **Decide who publishes and who reviews.** Even if there are only two of you, splitting the tasks avoids mistakes.

## Spreadsheet, content manager or your own app

A spreadsheet works for the inventory, but it doesn't present content attractively. A generic [[CMS|cms]] helps with publishing, though it usually forces recipes into the shape of a blog post. An app designed for food content starts from the recipe and the ingredient as its building blocks, with an admin dashboard to manage them and visual cards to present them.

## Think about who will see it

Internal order shows on the outside. Well-structured cards make for a good [[user experience|ux]], read well on mobile thanks to [[responsive|responsive]] design and help with [[SEO|seo]], because each recipe or product has its own page with clear information. On an island with as many visitors as Tenerife, it's also worth considering whether the content should be offered in several languages.

## Where to start tomorrow

Pick ten recipes or products, the most visited or the ones you're asked about most, and move them to the new structure. That will show you whether the categories and fields work before migrating the rest.
