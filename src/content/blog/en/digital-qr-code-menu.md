The [[digital menu|carta-digital]] has become a fixture in many restaurants and bars, but they don't all work equally well. Some menus can be read at a glance, while others force you to zoom in on a PDF on your phone. The difference lies in thinking of it as a small website, not as a photo of the paper menu.

## What it must include

- **Clear categories**: starters, mains, desserts, drinks. Customers should be able to jump to each one without scrolling through the whole menu.
- **Name, short description and price** for every dish, always up to date.
- **Allergens** clearly shown on every dish, with a key that's easy to understand.
- **Photos** of the main dishes, real and well lit.
- **Languages**, if you have customers from abroad.
- **Practical information**: whether bread or service is charged separately, vegetarian or gluten-free options, the set menu of the day.

## The QR code

The [[QR code|codigo-qr]] should be somewhere visible on the table, large enough and with a short line of text explaining what it's for. Using a dynamic code lets you change the menu without reprinting the table cards. Check that it scans properly with different phones and in the real lighting of your premises, at night too.

## Common mistakes

1. **Uploading a PDF of the printed menu**: it forces people to zoom in and is slow to load.
2. **Different prices** on the digital menu, the website and the [[POS|tpv]]. It's the most common complaint and the easiest to avoid by syncing them.
3. **Sold-out dishes that still appear**, which leads to orders that can't be fulfilled.
4. **Menus that won't load** on a weak connection, because of too many heavy images.
5. **Not offering an alternative**: some customers prefer paper, so always keep a few printed menus to hand.

## Designed for mobile

Text that can be read without zooming, large buttons, good contrast and simple navigation. The menu is part of your image: the colours, typeface and photos should be consistent with your [[visual identity|identidad-visual]].

## Keeping it up to date effortlessly

The biggest problem with digital menus is that someone has to update them. If they're fed directly from the POS, every price change or sold-out dish is reflected automatically, and the menu stops being another job on the to-do list.
