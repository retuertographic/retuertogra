Anyone visiting a new place usually has their information scattered about: a PDF leaflet, a video on social media, a website with opening hours and a map in another app. None of it knows where the visitor is at that moment. A geolocated video guide brings everything together in one place and organises it on a map, so that each piece of content appears next to the place it belongs to.

## What exactly it is

It's a digital guide used on a mobile phone that links three things: **videos, places and useful information**. On an interactive map with [[geolocation|geolocalizacion]], visitors can see where they are and what's nearby. Tapping a point opens its profile: a video filmed at that very spot, a description, how to get there, and recommendations or related services.

Places can be grouped into routes and points of interest, content can be offered in several languages, and everything is managed from an admin dashboard, without needing a technician to change a text or add a video.

## What problem it solves

- **Scattered information**: one guide instead of several disconnected channels.
- **Videos watched at the wrong moment**: a video about a viewpoint makes more sense when you're standing at the viewpoint than on your sofa at home.
- **Visitors from many countries**: in destinations such as Tenerife, offering content in each visitor's language makes a real difference.
- **Content that goes out of date**: a printed leaflet can't be updated; a guide with a management dashboard can.

## When it makes sense

It's a particularly good fit in these cases:

1. **Councils and tourist offices** that want to show off their area's heritage, neighbourhoods or beaches in a more visual way.
2. **Natural sites** with trails and viewpoints, where a route on the map helps people find their way.
3. **Museums and cultural centres** that want to extend the visit with videos linked to each room or exhibit.
4. **Tourism businesses** that run excursions or tours and want to offer their customers digital support along the way.

If you only have one or two places to show, a good website is probably enough. The guide pays off when there are several points spread across an area and audiovisual content to tell about them.

## What you need before you start

- A list of places with their locations.
- Videos already filmed, or a plan to film them.
- Basic texts for each site and the languages you want to publish them in.
- Someone on the team responsible for keeping the content up to date.

As most visits will come from phones, the design has to be [[responsive|responsive]] and the [[user experience|ux]] very simple: open, see the map and tap a point. The guide can also be connected to your website and social media so it's easy to reach.
