A website isn't a brochure you print once. It's software that depends on a server, a content management system, add-ons and external services that change all the time. When nobody looks after it, problems don't announce themselves: a form stops sending emails, an old add-on opens the door to an attack, or the page takes so long to load that the customer leaves.

## The basic maintenance tasks

- **Security updates** for the [[CMS|cms]], the theme and every [[plugin|plugin]]. Most hacked websites are attacked through old versions with known flaws.
- **Regular [[backups|copia-seguridad]]**, stored outside the server itself and tested now and then. A backup that has never been restored offers no guarantee.
- **Form checks**: making sure messages really arrive. It's one of the most common failures and one of the costliest, because nobody notices.
- **Bug fixing**: broken links, pages that don't load or elements out of place on mobile.
- **Monitoring**: alerts if the site goes down or the [[SSL certificate|ssl]] is about to expire.
- **Content updates**: prices, opening hours, photos and text kept current.

## What happens when it's neglected

The effects tend to arrive together. The site gets slower and drops in Google. Browser security warnings appear and scare visitors away. And if an attack or a serious [[hosting|hosting]] failure strikes without a recent backup, recovering the site can cost far more than years of maintenance.

## How often to check each thing

| Task | Indicative frequency |
| --- | --- |
| Security updates | Weekly or as soon as they are released |
| Backups | Daily or weekly, depending on how often the site changes |
| Form tests | Monthly and after every change |
| Speed and error review | Monthly |
| [[Domain|dominio]] and certificate renewal | Check before each expiry date |

## Do it yourself or delegate

If you have the time and knowledge, you can handle some of these tasks. But maintenance has one problem: it's easy to put off until something breaks. Delegating it gives you a fixed routine, someone who responds when there's a problem and peace of mind that your website keeps working for you.
