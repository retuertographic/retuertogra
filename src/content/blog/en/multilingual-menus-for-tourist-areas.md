In destinations like Tenerife, a significant share of a restaurant's customers don't speak Spanish. A menu that's only in one language, or that has confusing translations, leads to doubts, dishes that don't get ordered for fear of getting it wrong and more work for the front-of-house staff. A good multilingual menu solves much of that problem before the waiter even reaches the table.

## Which languages to choose

Start with your real customers: ask the front-of-house team where most of them come from or look through your reviews. In many tourist areas, Spanish, English and German cover a large proportion of visitors, and you can add another language or two depending on the season. It's better to have three languages translated well than seven full of mistakes.

## Translating isn't just swapping words

- **The names of traditional dishes** often aren't translated: "papas arrugadas" stays as it is, with a brief explanation of what they are.
- **Descriptions** should explain the ingredients and how the dish is prepared in words a visitor will understand.
- **Allergens** must appear in every language with the same accuracy. A mistake here isn't a matter of style.
- **Avoid unchecked machine translations**: they tend to produce confusing or downright comical results.

## Let the customer choose their language

On a [[digital menu|carta-digital]], the language should be selectable on arrival, with a visible language switcher. If the customer's phone is set to another language, the menu can suggest that language automatically, but it should always let them change it.

## The challenge: keeping every version up to date

With three languages, every change to a price or a dish has to be made three times. It's easy for one version to fall behind and for the price in English not to match the one on the bill. The solution is to have a single source of data: the [[POS|tpv]] holds prices and availability, and each language only adds its own text. Through an [[integration|integracion]], a price change reaches every version at once.

## Beyond the menu

If your restaurant welcomes a lot of foreign visitors, also check that your website, your Google Business Profile and your replies to reviews are available in the main languages. The experience begins before anyone sits down at the table.
