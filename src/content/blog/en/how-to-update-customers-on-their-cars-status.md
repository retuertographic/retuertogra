"Is my car ready yet?" It's the most repeated question in any body and paint shop. Every call interrupts someone who was working, means going to check the vehicle and often ends with "we'll ring you when it's done". Customers don't call out of mistrust: they call because they have no information. If you give it to them before they ask, you save time and they get peace of mind.

## The moments when customers want to know something

You don't need to report every bolt. The milestones that really matter are enough:

1. **Intake confirmed.** The car is in and registered.
2. **Quote ready.** They need to review and approve it.
3. **Repair under way.** Work on their vehicle has started.
4. **Surprise or extension.** Hidden damage has appeared and a decision is needed.
5. **Ready for collection.** With the day and time to pick it up.

Five well-timed updates prevent most calls.

## How to write the updates

- **Short and specific**: registration or model, status and next step.
- **With a date where there is one**, even an approximate one.
- **In a friendly tone**, the same one you'd use at the counter.
- **No jargon.** "We're painting the door" is clearer than the name of the part.

When you communicate by chat, it's best to use a business account such as [[WhatsApp Business|whatsapp-business]] rather than an employee's personal phone, so conversations don't depend on one person.

## Deliveries, pick-ups and courtesy vehicles

Much of the end-of-day chaos comes from here: several customers arriving at once, a courtesy car that hasn't come back or a pick-up nobody wrote down. A few guidelines:

- Book delivery slots and don't let them overlap.
- Record which courtesy vehicle each customer has and when it's due back.
- Every morning, check the day's deliveries and confirm with customers.

## From manual updates to automatic ones

Doing it by hand works with a few cars, but it relies on someone remembering. When the repair status is updated in an app, the customer notification can go out at that very moment. That [[automation|automatizacion]] doesn't remove the personal touch: it saves it for when it's needed, such as explaining an extended quote. And with the status of every car on an internal dashboard, anyone in the team can answer without walking to the bay. Good [[user experience|ux]] on that dashboard means updating a status takes seconds.

## How to tell whether things have improved

For a couple of weeks, count the calls asking about a car's status. Repeat the count after introducing updates. That's the clearest [[KPI|kpi]], along with the number of late handovers.
