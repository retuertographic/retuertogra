A custom application doesn't start with writing code, but with properly understanding the work it has to make easier. The better prepared you are for the first meeting, the faster things move and the fewer surprises crop up later. This guide sums up how to organise the project, which mistakes to avoid and how to tell whether it was worth it.

## Step by step

1. **Describe the current process as it really is.** Who does what, with which tools and where it gets stuck. A hand-drawn diagram or a list of steps is enough.
2. **Choose one main goal.** For example: "requests arrive complete" or "we always know the status of every job". A clear goal helps decide what goes into the first version.
3. **Identify the user roles.** Customers, staff, managers or admin: each will have different permissions and screens.
4. **Gather existing data and systems.** Customer lists, catalogues, the [[CRM|crm]] or the website that need connecting through an [[API|api]].
5. **Validate a prototype.** Before development, review some sample screens with the people who will use the app. It's the cheapest moment to change your mind.
6. **Build a focused first version.** An application that does the essentials well is better than one that tries to do everything at once.
7. **Test with real users and launch.** A trial period with part of the team uncovers problems you won't spot from the office.

## Common mistakes

- **Trying to fit everything into the first version.** It stretches timelines and makes the app harder to use. Improvements can come later.
- **Designing without the people who'll use it.** If the field team isn't involved, the app may be correct on paper and awkward in practice. Good [[UX|ux]] is built by listening.
- **Forgetting about personal data.** If the app stores customer or employee data, [[GDPR|rgpd]] has to be considered from the design stage: who can access what, and how long it's kept.
- **Not planning for change.** Every tool needs adjustments once people start using it. Agree from the outset how those will be handled.

## How to measure whether it's working

Go back to the goal from step 2 and compare before and after. Some simple [[KPIs|kpi]]:

| Indicator | What it tells you |
| --- | --- |
| Average time per request | Whether the process has sped up |
| Incomplete or incorrect requests | Whether the forms capture what's needed |
| Actual use of the app | Whether the team has adopted it or stuck with the old method |

Review these figures one month and three months after launch. They'll show what works and what to adjust in the next version.
