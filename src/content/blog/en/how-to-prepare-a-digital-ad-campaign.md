Launching an ad takes minutes. Preparing it properly takes a bit longer, but that's what makes the difference between a campaign that brings in customers and one that just eats budget. This is a practical guide to organising a campaign from start to finish, whether you run it yourself or hand it over.

## 1. Set a single goal

Each campaign should pursue one thing: calls, bookings, quote requests, online sales or visits to your premises. If you mix goals, you won't know what worked. Write it in one sentence: "We want table bookings for weekday evenings".

## 2. Describe who you're targeting

- Where do they live or where are they? For example, residents of the metropolitan area or visitors staying in the south of the island.
- What do they need, or what problem do they have?
- What would make them choose you over someone else?

The more specific the description, the easier it is to target and to write the message.

## 3. Prepare the offer and the message

A good campaign gives a clear reason to act now: something new, a season, a specific service. The message should say in a few words what you offer, to whom and what the person needs to do. Always end with a direct [[call to action|cta]]: "Book now", "Ask for a quote", "Message us".

## 4. Design the creatives

1. **Consistent with your brand.** Colours, typefaces and logo in line with your [[visual identity|identidad-visual]], so the ad is recognisable.
2. **Adapted to each format.** The same idea needs vertical, square and landscape versions depending on where it appears.
3. **Light on text.** The image or video tells the story; the text clarifies.
4. **With alternatives.** Prepare two or three versions of the ad to compare which performs better.

If you have your own photos or video of your business, use them: they show what you really offer.

## 5. Prepare the destination

The ad only brings the person in; the page convinces them. Send them to a [[landing page|landing-page]] that talks about exactly what the ad promised, loads quickly on mobile and has the form or contact button in plain view. Also check that your website's privacy policy and cookie notice are up to date, in line with the [[GDPR|rgpd]].

## 6. Set up measurement

Before launching, make sure what matters is recorded: form submissions, calls, bookings or purchases. Without this, any later analysis is guesswork. Decide too which [[KPIs|kpi]] you'll review.

## 7. Set a test budget and duration

Start with a budget you can afford and a period long enough to gather data. Don't change everything every day: give the campaign time before drawing conclusions.

## 8. Launch, review and adjust

Review the results regularly, pause what isn't working and reinforce what is.
