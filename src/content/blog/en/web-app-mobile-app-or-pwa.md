When a business decides to digitise a process, one of the first questions is what type of application it needs. "I want an app" can mean very different things, and choosing the right format from the start avoids redoing work later on. These are the three usual options and when each one fits.

## Web application

It's used in the browser, with nothing to install. It works on computers, tablets and phones, provided it's properly adapted to each screen (what's known as [[responsive|responsive]] design). It's the most common choice for management dashboards, private customer areas or internal tools used mainly from the office.

**It fits if** your users work mostly on a computer and you want anyone to get in with a link and their login.

## Mobile app

It's downloaded from the app stores and installed on the phone. It has more direct access to the device's features and is designed for daily use on mobile. In return, it has to be published in the stores and its versions kept up to date.

**It fits if** use is intensive on mobile and you need to make full use of the phone's features.

## PWA

A [[PWA|pwa]] (progressive web app) sits halfway between the two: it opens in the browser, but it can be added to the home screen and behaves much like an installed app. It can send notifications and, depending on how it's built, keep working with a limited connection.

**It fits if** you want an app-like experience on mobile without relying on the stores, and the same tool to work on a computer too.

## Quick comparison

| Aspect | Web app | Mobile app | PWA |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Installation | Not needed | From the store | Optional, from the browser |
| Devices | Computer and mobile | Mobile | Computer and mobile |
| Updates | Immediate | New version in the store | Immediate |
| Typical use | Dashboards and admin | Daily use on mobile | Teams on the move |

## How to decide in three steps

1. **Think about who uses it and where.** A field team reporting from different places around Tenerife has different needs from an admin team in the office.
2. **Check which features you need**: notifications, camera, [[geolocation|geolocalizacion]] or offline work.
3. **Consider maintenance.** The more separate versions, the more updating work. A good [[user experience|ux]] in a single format is usually better than three half-finished ones.

In many projects there's no single answer: a web dashboard for the office and a PWA for people working out and about, connected to the same data.
