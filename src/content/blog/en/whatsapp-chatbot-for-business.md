WhatsApp has become the channel many customers prefer to write on: they ask for a price, check whether there is a free appointment or send a photo of what they need. The trouble starts when messages pile up, get answered late or get lost among personal chats. A well-designed [[chatbot|chatbot]] doesn't replace your team: it frees them from repetitive questions and keeps everything else organised.

## What a WhatsApp chatbot can do

- **Answer frequent questions instantly**: opening hours, location, indicative prices, lead times or the documents required.
- **Collect the details of a request** before a person steps in: name, service of interest, dates or photos.
- **Sort conversations** by type (quote, appointment, issue) so each one reaches the right person.
- **Reply outside working hours** and leave the enquiry ready for the next day, instead of a silence that makes the customer look elsewhere.
- **Hand the conversation over to a person** when needed, with the full context in view.

## What a person should keep doing

A chatbot works well with predictable questions. Delicate complaints, price negotiations or unusual cases need human judgement. The key design decision is when the bot steps aside and alerts the team. A good chatbot knows when to say "I'll put you through to a colleague".

## Why connect it to a CRM

Answering quickly is only half the job. The other half is not losing the opportunity. If every conversation is logged in a [[CRM|crm]], with the contact, what they asked for and where it stands, you can follow up, see which enquiries turn into sales and share the work among several people without getting in each other's way. To use WhatsApp with several agents and automations you work with the [[WhatsApp Business|whatsapp-business]] platform connected to your software, not with the office mobile phone.

## How to launch it step by step

1. **Gather the real questions** you receive over two or three weeks. They define what the bot must be able to answer.
2. **Define the flows**: which details to ask for in each type of enquiry and when to hand over to a person.
3. **Write the answers in your own tone**. A bot that sounds like your business builds trust.
4. **Connect your CRM, email or forms** through an [[integration|integracion]] so nothing is copied by hand.
5. **Review the conversations during the first month** and adjust what doesn't work.

## How to tell whether it's working

Pick a few indicators and check them monthly: time to first reply, share of enquiries resolved without a person, and number of requests that become quotes. With those three [[KPIs|kpi]] you'll know whether the chatbot saves time and helps you sell.
