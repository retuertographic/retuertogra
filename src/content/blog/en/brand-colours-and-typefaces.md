Colours and typefaces make a brand recognisable even before anyone reads its name. For a small business, choosing them well isn't a matter of personal taste: what counts is that they work across every format, convey what you want to say and stay consistent over time.

## Colours

A brand palette usually has three levels:

- **Primary colour**: the one that identifies the brand and appears in the logo and the most visible elements.
- **Secondary colours**: one or two that complement it and allow variety without losing consistency.
- **Neutrals**: whites, greys and blacks for text and backgrounds, which are the ones used most from day to day.

When choosing them, think about your sector and your competitors: standing out helps people remember you. Check the contrast too: light grey text on white may look elegant, but if it can't be read, it's no use. Web accessibility guidelines recommend a minimum contrast between text and background, and following them improves the [[user experience|ux]] for everyone.

## Screen and print

The same colour is defined differently depending on where it's used: RGB and hexadecimal for screens, CMYK for print and, if precision is needed, a Pantone reference. Set out the equivalents from the start and bear in mind that some colours that look very vivid on screen can't be reproduced in the same way on paper.

## Typefaces

Two families are usually enough: one for headings, with more personality, and another for body text, highly legible. When choosing them:

1. **Test legibility** at small sizes and on mobile.
2. **Check the licence**: it must allow use on the web, in print and, where relevant, in apps.
3. **Make sure it has the characters you need**: accents, the Spanish ñ, German umlauts and other symbols in the languages you communicate in.
4. **Choose enough weights** (regular, semibold, bold) to create hierarchy without resorting to other fonts.
5. **Define fallbacks** for when the brand typeface isn't available, for example in office documents.

## Write the decisions down

All of this should be recorded in the [[brand guidelines|manual-marca]], with usage examples and permitted combinations. That way, whoever designs a presentation, a post or a sign will have the rules to hand and your [[visual identity|identidad-visual]] will stay consistent.
